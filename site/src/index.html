Abstract

An idle superconducting qubit waiting during a neighbour's mid-circuit measurement loses coherence to stochastic dephasing and to deterministic frequency shifts. We ask whether model-based optimization of dynamical-decoupling (DD) pulse timings and phases with CMA-ES outperforms strong canonical sequences. To answer it fairly, we pre-registered a synthetic transmon-like model, seeds, baselines and a training-only baseline-selection rule, and scored every arm on independent held-out noise. A finite-pulse Monte-Carlo simulator agrees with filter-function theory in five predetermined configurations (largest |z| = @@num:GthreeMaxZ@@). Synthetic Ramsey/Hahn/CPMG spectroscopy recovers a predictive noise model (held-out RMSE @@num:SpecGaussFullRmseRatio@@× the shot-noise floor), but the spectrum is not uniquely identified. In the pre-registered Scenario A, no CMA-ES mode delivered a practically meaningful gain over the selected XY4/XY8 baseline, whose infidelity (@@num:BenchASixteenSelEps@@ at n = 16) is already at the T1-plus-white-noise floor (@@num:FloorA@@). With a deterministic Stark-like shift and virtual-Z correction, CMA-ES beat XY4 by a few percent at the nominal profile only at n = 4. The timing+phase variant of that gain reversed under the pre-registered ±100 ns readout-timing shifts, and only phase-only sequences kept a marginal, model-specific advantage. Optimizing on fitted models, or with more headroom, did not help, and training gains did not generalize. All results are synthetic; no quantum hardware was used.

1Introduction and research questions

Motivation. The project originates from a course project-ideas brief @@cite:QMBrief2026@@; that brief is not evidence that this implementation exists or that hardware access has been provisioned. Prospective use of IQCC hardware remains unconfirmed: no access, authorization or hardware run exists for this report.

Problem. An idle qubit accumulates phase errors from stochastic dephasing and, in dynamic circuits, from deterministic frequency shifts during neighbouring readout. We ask whether model-based optimization of DD sequences can reduce idle-window infidelity relative to strong canonical baselines under a fixed pulse budget and identical constraints.

RQ1
Agreement of Monte-Carlo coherence with analytic filter-function predictions under matched PSD conventions.
RQ2
Predictive (held-out) accuracy of noise parameters fitted from synthetic Ramsey, Hahn and CPMG data.
RQ3
Whether joint optimization of pulse timings and phases beats strong DD baselines in Scenario A (stochastic dephasing) on held-out noise.
RQ4
Whether any gain over calibrated virtual-Z and standard DD survives uncertainty in readout-induced detuning (Scenario B).
RQ5
Robustness to pulse imperfections, drift, calibration error, PSD misspecification and control quantization.

Scope. Synthetic simulations only. Scenarios, seeds, baselines, the baseline-selection rule and the metrics were pre-registered in the repository (docs/EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md) before any optimization run; deviations are logged. A zero gain or a regression counts as a valid outcome. Metric: average gate infidelity against the corrected target (Section 3).

2Related work

Dynamical decoupling of open quantum systems was introduced by Viola, Knill and Lloyd @@cite:Viola1999@@. Uhrig proposed optimized π-pulse sequences for keeping a qubit coherent @@cite:Uhrig2007@@, and Cywiński et al. analysed how to enhance dephasing times in superconducting qubits @@cite:Cywinski2008@@. Biercuk et al. studied optimized DD in a model quantum memory @@cite:Biercuk2009@@. DD has also been used for noise spectroscopy, with a superconducting flux qubit @@cite:Bylander2011@@ and for colored noise @@cite:Alvarez2011@@. Robust DD was studied by Souza, Álvarez and Suter @@cite:Souza2011@@, and Ezzell et al. surveyed DD performance on superconducting qubits @@cite:Ezzell2023@@. Learning-based approaches to choosing DD sequences include @@cite:Rahman2024,Tong2025,Tong2026@@.

Empirical versus model-based. Of the learning-based works, the title of @@cite:Tong2025@@ states its setting: learning is empirical and carried out on quantum processors, i.e. hardware in the loop. The published abstract of @@cite:Tong2026@@ describes empirical optimization of DD in dynamic quantum circuits, including measurement and feedforward. The present work, by contrast, is synthetic and model-based.

Positioning. Sequences are optimized and evaluated against a simulated noise model; no quantum hardware has been used. The contribution is limited to a pre-registered, held-out synthetic benchmark of model-based DD optimization with explicit noise floors. Its main finding is negative, and a synthetic benchmark of this kind does not test whether its conclusions transfer to hardware.

3Physical model and conventions

We use \hbar = 1, time in s, angular frequency and detuning in rad/s, and angles in rad. In the rotating frame of the drive,

H(t) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\delta(t) + d(t) + \delta_{\mathrm{off}}\bigr]\sigma_z + \tfrac{1}{2}\Omega(t)\bigl[\cos\varphi(t)\,\sigma_x + \sin\varphi(t)\,\sigma_y\bigr]
(1)

where \delta(t) is zero-mean stationary noise, assumed Gaussian unless stated otherwise, d(t) a known deterministic detuning profile (e.g. during neighbouring readout), and \delta_{\mathrm{off}} a static calibration offset.

Spectrum and filter

With autocorrelation C(\tau)=\langle\delta(t+\tau)\,\delta(t)\rangle we use the two-sided PSD in rad²/s,

S_2(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} C(\tau)\,e^{-i\omega\tau}\,d\tau .
(2)

For the switching function y(t) (toggling-frame z component, \pm1 between ideal π pulses),

Y(\omega)=\int_0^T y(t)\,e^{i\omega t}\,dt, \qquad \chi=\operatorname{Var}\phi=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} S_2(\omega)\,|Y(\omega)|^2\,d\omega ,
(3)

and, under the Gaussian assumption, W=e^{-\chi/2} with pure-dephasing average gate infidelity r=(1-W)/3. Low- and high-frequency cutoffs of S_2 are stated explicitly for every model.

Energy relaxation

T_1 is modelled as amplitude damping with \gamma = 1-e^{-t/T_1} per free segment; channels are composed as Pauli transfer matrices, never by adding infidelities. Amplitude damping alone has average infidelity

r_{\mathrm{AD}}=\frac{2+\gamma-2\sqrt{1-\gamma}}{6}\approx\frac{\gamma}{3},
(4)

derived in docs/PHYSICS.md and checked numerically against Haar and six-state averages. An expression [3-2\sqrt{1-\gamma}-\gamma]/6 that appeared in the project plan gives 1/6 at \gamma=0 and was rejected.

Target and frame correction

Fidelity is evaluated against U_{\mathrm{target}}=R_z(\theta_{\mathrm{vz}})\,U_0, where U_0 is the ideal net control unitary of the sequence and \theta_{\mathrm{vz}} is a virtual-Z correction of the known d(t) (zero unless frame correction is allowed). Average gate fidelity of a channel with PTM R is

F_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}R'+2}{6},\qquad R' = R_{\mathrm{target}}^{\mathsf T}R .
(5)

For finite pulses we use the generalized (three-component toggling-frame) filter function, so W=e^{-\chi/2} is exact only for instantaneous pulses and leading-order otherwise; the Monte-Carlo simulator needs neither approximation. Finite pulse widths, minimum spacing and timing-grid constraints are enforced for all sequences.

4Methods

Synthetic device (Scenario A). All device numbers are synthetic assumptions, fixed in a pre-registered plan before any optimization run. The idle window is T = 2\,\mu\mathrm{s} (alternate 4 µs), with square π pulses of width w = 32 ns, a 4 ns guard and pulse starts on a 4 ns grid (an assumed controller tick). The dephasing PSD is the sum of a band-limited 1/f term (2\pi\cdot1 Hz – 2\pi\cdot10 MHz, scaled so that the analytic Ramsey coherence is e^{-1} at 10 µs), a Gaussian Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (Lorentzian) term with \tau_c = 0.5\,\mu\mathrm{s}, and a white floor S_0 = 10^4 rad²/s; T_1 = 100\,\mu\mathrm{s}; and each trajectory draws a relative Rabi-amplitude error \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,0.02^2). Scenario B adds a deterministic Stark-like shift of 2\pi\cdot0.2 MHz between 0.6 and 1.6 µs (20 ns tanh edges) as a stand-in for a neighbouring readout; it models a known frequency shift only, not measurement-induced dephasing.

Finite-pulse Monte-Carlo simulator. Gaussian noise trajectories are sampled on a 1 ns grid by spectral synthesis (exact Ornstein–Uhlenbeck recursion for the Lorentzian; exact Markov jumps for telegraph noise). Free segments are exact z rotations by the integrated detuning; pulses are propagated in 8 piecewise-constant sub-steps, including detuning during the pulse. Amplitude damping is composed per free segment and approximated as \mathrm{AD}(w/2)\,U\,\mathrm{AD}(w/2) during pulses. The noise-averaged PTM gives F_{\mathrm{avg}} against the corrected target; the reported \epsilon is the mean of per-trajectory infidelities, which equals the channel infidelity because F_{\mathrm{avg}} is linear in the channel.

Spectroscopy and held-out fits. Synthetic Ramsey, Hahn, CPMG-2 and CPMG-4 data (14 log-spaced idle times in [0.1, 40] µs, 1000 shots per point, binomial shot noise, an affine SPAM map) are fitted by binomial maximum likelihood with a parametric bootstrap. Two Gaussian models are fitted: reduced (1/f with \alpha=1, plus white) and full (reduced plus Lorentzian). T_1 is treated as known from a separate experiment, because white dephasing and 1/T_1 are degenerate for instantaneous pulses. Held-out data are CPMG-8 and CPMG-16 at all times, and the training families at 13 unseen times, drawn with an independent generator; the fit never sees held-out observations. A misspecification control replaces the Lorentzian with telegraph noise of equal second-order statistics.

CMA-ES optimization. We use pycma 4.5.0 @@cite:Hansen2016@@, an evolution strategy rather than reinforcement learning. Timings are encoded by n unconstrained coordinates,

g_k = g_{\min} + \bigl(T - n w - (n+1)\,g_{\min}\bigr)\,\operatorname{softmax}([0,z])_k ,
(6)

with no redundant shift; starts are snapped to the grid and candidates that cannot be repaired are rejected. Pulse phases are offsets from the base phases, projected so that the ideal net unitary stays equal to the base sequence's, so fidelity is never graded against the wrong target. Three modes are run: timing-only and phase-only (both starting from CPMG with Y pulses) and timing+phase. Each run has a budget of 2000 objective evaluations on 256 frozen training trajectories (common random numbers), with three independent training runs (training seeds 101–103, optimizer seeds 1–3).

Baselines, selection rule and evaluation. At each n, the baselines are CPMG-n (Y pulses), XY4, XY8 (where n divides), UDD-n and free evolution, with the same T, w, guard, grid, noise model and frame-correction privileges. The strongest baseline is fixed by a pre-registered rule: the lowest training infidelity. All arms are then evaluated on the same held-out test draws (seeds 9001–9005, 1024 trajectories each; disjointness from training is enforced in code). We report raw \epsilon, the ratio \epsilon_{\mathrm{selected}}/\epsilon_{\mathrm{CMA}} (values > 1 favour CMA-ES), and a two-stage paired-bootstrap 95% interval. These intervals quantify Monte-Carlo uncertainty within the synthetic model only; they are not significance tests and do not account for model selection or model mismatch.

6Discussion, limitations and roadmap

Interpretation. Under the pre-registered model the answer to RQ3 is negative. XY4/XY8 already suppress the decouplable colored noise, and the remaining infidelity is set by T1 and white dephasing, which no π-pulse sequence can remove. Where headroom exists (the colored-only ablation), our CMA-ES setup still did not beat XY8 on held-out noise. Its training improvements came from fitting the finite set of frozen trajectories, and the timing+phase search starting from CPMG did not reach XY8's robustness to amplitude errors within 2000 evaluations. These are statements about this parameterization, budget and synthetic model, not about CMA-ES or learned DD in general. The one positive signal (Scenario B, n = 4) is a few percent and was identified after the fact; its timing+phase form does not survive readout-timing uncertainty, and its phase-only form does only within this model. It would need a new pre-registered test before any claim.

Limitations

  • All evidence is synthetic. Device parameters are assumptions, not measurements, and optimizing against a model one also evaluates on is circular; the fitted-model pipeline shows that identification error only adds loss.
  • Noise is Gaussian and stationary (except in the telegraph control, which is near-Gaussian at our parameters). Non-stationary, measurement-induced dephasing during readout is not modelled; Scenario B is a known deterministic frequency shift only.
  • T1 during pulses is approximated; leakage, crosstalk, finite bandwidth and pulse shaping are absent. Pulse errors are a static per-trajectory amplitude error.
  • Monte-Carlo intervals capture sampling uncertainty within the model only; with 5 test seeds the seed-level variability is coarsely resolved. The colored-only, no-T1, no-amplitude-error and convergence analyses were added after seeing the pre-registered results and are exploratory. One plumbing smoke run touched the test seeds before the main run (documented in docs/EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md).
  • Robustness of the optimized sequences to calibration drift, Stark rise time and timing-grid quantization (plan item P5.2) was not evaluated. The exploratory ablations reused the held-out test seeds, and the spectroscopy and benchmark bootstraps share one seed.
  • Earlier runs recorded the git state at manifest write time rather than run start; several exploratory runs were marked dirty without preserving their uncommitted diffs. Provenance now records the start-of-run git state and diff for future runs, which cannot retroactively recover earlier uncommitted code (docs/REVIEW_FINDINGS.md).
  • Literature records were checked at identity level against arXiv; publisher pagination and DOIs are not fully audited (docs/CITATION_AUDIT.md).
  • No quantum-hardware execution was performed. The QUA adapter is an SDK-free schedule translator marked not hardware tested; the QM SDK serialization test is skipped because the SDK is not installed in the project environment.

Independent review. An AI red-team pass (docs/REVIEW_FINDINGS.md) independently re-derived the conventions, reproduced several reported numbers bit-for-bit and found no critical defect. Its high-severity finding (the Scenario B timing+phase gain is not robust) is incorporated above. Human review of the evidence ledger is pending.

Roadmap. (i) Measure, with authorization, T1, Ramsey, Hahn and CPMG decays and spectator phase during neighbour readout on the target device (docs/HARDWARE_PROTOCOL.md), to see whether the decouplable colored component is large enough for optimization to matter at all. (ii) Model measurement-induced dephasing as non-stationary noise and repeat Scenario B. (iii) Regularize the optimizer (more training trajectories, robust objectives, starting from XY8) and pre-register a new held-out benchmark.
