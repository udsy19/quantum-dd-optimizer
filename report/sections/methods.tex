\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
% Describes only what the code implements (src/ddopt/*). All numbers below come from
% configs/scenario_a.json (synthetic assumptions) or generated macros.

\paragraph{Synthetic device (Scenario~A).} All device numbers are \emph{synthetic assumptions},
fixed in a pre-registered plan (\texttt{docs/EXPERIMENT\_PLAN.md}) before any optimization run.
The idle window is $T=2\,\mu$s (alternate $4\,\mu$s), with square $\pi$ pulses of width
$w=32$\,ns, a 4\,ns guard and pulse starts on a 4\,ns grid (an assumed controller tick).
The dephasing PSD is the sum of a band-limited $1/f$ term ($2\pi\cdot1$\,Hz--$2\pi\cdot10$\,MHz,
scaled so that the analytic Ramsey coherence is $e^{-1}$ at $10\,\mu$s), a Gaussian
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck (Lorentzian) term with $\tau_c=0.5\,\mu$s, and a white floor
$S_0=10^4$\,rad$^2$/s; $T_1=100\,\mu$s; and each trajectory draws a relative Rabi-amplitude error
$\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,0.02^2)$. Scenario~B adds a deterministic Stark-like shift of
$2\pi\cdot0.2$\,MHz between 0.6 and 1.6\,$\mu$s (20\,ns tanh edges) as a stand-in for a
neighbouring readout; it models a known frequency shift only, not measurement-induced
dephasing.

\paragraph{Finite-pulse Monte-Carlo simulator.} Gaussian noise trajectories are sampled on a
1\,ns grid by spectral synthesis (exact Ornstein--Uhlenbeck recursion for the Lorentzian; exact
Markov jumps for telegraph noise). Free segments are exact $z$ rotations by the integrated
detuning; pulses are propagated in 8 piecewise-constant sub-steps, including detuning during the
pulse. Amplitude damping is composed per free segment (exact, as it is phase-covariant) and
approximated as $\mathrm{AD}(w/2)\,U\,\mathrm{AD}(w/2)$ during pulses. The noise-averaged Pauli
transfer matrix gives $F_{\rm avg}$ against the corrected target. The reported $\epsilon$ is the
mean of per-trajectory infidelities, which equals the channel infidelity because $F_{\rm avg}$ is
linear in the channel. Unit tests check unitarity ($<10^{-14}$), zero-noise compensated infidelity
($\le10^{-10}$ for all families, odd and even $n$), $O(\Delta t^2)$ and $O(m^{-2})$ convergence
in noise step and pulse sub-steps, and the $T_1$-only formula to $10^{-12}$.

\paragraph{Spectroscopy and held-out fits.} Synthetic Ramsey, Hahn, CPMG-2 and CPMG-4 data
(14 log-spaced idle times in $[0.1,40]\,\mu$s, 1000 shots per point, binomial shot noise, an
affine SPAM map) are fitted by binomial maximum likelihood with a parametric bootstrap.
Two Gaussian models are fitted: \emph{reduced} ($1/f$ with $\alpha=1$, plus white) and
\emph{full} (reduced plus Lorentzian). $T_1$ is treated as known from a separate experiment,
because white dephasing and $1/T_1$ are degenerate for instantaneous pulses. Held-out data are
CPMG-8 and CPMG-16 at all times, and the training families at 13 unseen times, drawn with an
independent generator. The fit never sees held-out observations; a unit test asserts identical
parameters with and without test data. A misspecification control replaces the Lorentzian
with telegraph noise of equal second-order statistics.

\paragraph{CMA-ES optimization.} We use pycma 4.5.0 \citep{Hansen2016} (LIT-12), an evolution
strategy rather than reinforcement learning. Timings are encoded by $n$ unconstrained
coordinates, giving gaps $g_k=g_{\min}+(T-nw-(n+1)g_{\min})\,\mathrm{softmax}([0,z])_k$ with no
redundant shift; starts are snapped to the grid, and candidates that cannot be repaired are
rejected. Pulse phases are offsets from the base phases, projected so that the ideal net unitary
stays equal to the base sequence's ($\propto I$ or a Pauli). Fidelity is therefore never graded
against the wrong target. Three modes are run: timing-only and phase-only (both starting from
CPMG with $Y$ pulses) and timing+phase. Each run has a budget of 2000 objective evaluations on
256 frozen training trajectories (common random numbers). There are three independent training
runs (training seeds 101--103 with optimizer seeds 1--3).

\paragraph{Baselines, selection rule and evaluation.} At each $n$, the baselines are CPMG-$n$
($Y$ pulses), XY4, XY8 (where $n$ divides), UDD-$n$ and free evolution, with the same $T$, $w$,
guard, grid, noise model and frame-correction privileges. The \emph{strongest} baseline is fixed
by a pre-registered rule: the lowest \emph{training} infidelity. All arms are then evaluated on
the same held-out test draws (seeds 9001--9005, 1024 trajectories each; disjointness from
training is enforced in code). We report raw $\epsilon$, the ratio
$\epsilon_{\rm selected}/\epsilon_{\rm CMA}$ (values $>1$ favour CMA-ES), and a two-stage paired
bootstrap 95\% interval (seeds as blocks, trajectories within seeds). These intervals quantify
Monte-Carlo uncertainty within the synthetic model only; they are not significance tests and do
not account for model selection or model mismatch.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Each run writes an immutable \texttt{results/<run\_id>/} directory
with a manifest (UTC time, git SHA and dirty flag, platform, Python and package versions, config
hash, seeds, command) and SHA-256 checksums. Every table, figure and number macro in this report
is regenerated by \texttt{scripts/make\_report\_artifacts.py} from checksum-verified runs pinned
in \texttt{report/runs.json}; the claim ledger is \texttt{report/evidence\_map.csv}.
