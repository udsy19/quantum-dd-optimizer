\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}
All results in this section are computed on \emph{synthetic} device models; no IQCC
hardware run has been performed. The experiment configurations, raw outputs, seeds,
reproduction commands, checksums and claim ledger are in the public repository
(\repopath{results/}, \repopath{report/evidence\_map.csv}). Extended tables,
ablation plots and individual trial analyses are included in the supplementary
report (\repopath{report/sections/results\_full.tex}).

\subsection{Simulator and Spectroscopy Validation}
In five predetermined configurations, Monte Carlo infidelity agrees with the
ideal filter-function reference within the specified combined uncertainty
(largest standardized discrepancy $|z|=\GthreeMaxZ$); this establishes internal
consistency, \emph{not} device validation. The Gaussian fit predicts 106
held-out synthetic decay measurements (Fig.~\ref{fig:spec}) with RMSE
$\SpecGaussFullRmseRatio\times$ the shot-noise floor and 95\% interval coverage
$\SpecGaussFullCov$. These predictions rely on the same analytic forward
family used to generate the data, fixed $T_1$ and known cutoffs.
The spectral model is not uniquely identified.

\subsection{Scenario A: Canonical DD Versus Optimization}
With equal pulse count and independent held-out noise (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}), no CMA-ES mode provides
a practically meaningful improvement over the training-selected XY4/XY8
baseline. In particular, the $n=16$ baseline infidelity
$\BenchASixteenSelEps$ is close to the simulated $T_1$-plus-white-noise
floor $\FloorA$. The largest held-out ratio $\epsilon_{\rm sel}/\epsilon_{\rm CMA}$ is
$\BenchAFourBothRatioHi$ (one $n=4$ timing+phase run): gains are at most about 1\%, which
is not practically meaningful and does not establish optimization headroom. Joint timing/phase optimization fits frozen
training trajectories more closely while degrading on independent evaluation,
consistent with overfitting under the tested budget and model.

\subsection{Scenario B: Readout-Like Detuning}
A deterministic, synthetic Stark-like frequency profile was added to the
idle window. The experiment does not model stochastic measurement-induced
dephasing or measured readout. The joint timing/phase optimization offers a
few-percent nominal advantage over XY4 plus virtual-$Z$ at $n=4$, but this
reverses for prescribed $\pm100$\,ns timing shifts. The phase-only $n=4$
candidate retains a small advantage in the tested perturbation sweep.
These positive cells were selected after examining multiple comparisons;
they are exploratory and cannot support a general hardware claim.

\subsection{Ablations and Interpretation}
Removing the white-noise and relaxation limits creates additional theoretical
headroom, but under the specified search budget CMA-ES gained less than 0.5\% over
XY8 at $n=8$ in held-out evaluation (ratios up to $\AblColoredEightPhaseRatioHi$,
colored-only phase-only, and $\AblNoToneEightBothRatioHi$, no-$T_1$ timing+phase) and
lost substantially at $n=16$ (colored-only timing+phase, ratio
$\AblColoredSixteenBothRatioLo$--$\AblColoredSixteenBothRatioHi$). The fitted-model pipeline further exposes simulation
to model mismatch. Overall, canonical robust DD is a difficult baseline
to beat in the tested regimes; the synthetic results do not rule out
advantages from alternative noise spectra, optimizers, or real hardware.
